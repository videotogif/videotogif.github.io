How to convert a video to GIF

  1. Upload your video

    Drag & drop a video file or click to choose one. MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV and most formats your browser can play are supported. The file is read locally – it is never sent to a server.

  2. Check the frames

    Frames are extracted automatically and the GIF is generated right away, so you can preview exactly what the animation will look like.

    Click Download GIF to save the result. Need a shorter clip? Open Trim GIF to cut it down to the exact part you want.

Frequently asked questions

Is this video to GIF converter really free?

Yes. There are no limits, no sign-up and no watermark. The site is supported by ads.

Are my videos uploaded to a server?

No. Your video is decoded and encoded entirely inside your browser using WebAssembly. Your video and the resulting GIF are never sent over the network.

Which video formats are supported?

Any format your browser can play: MP4 (H.264), MOV, WebM, MKV and more. If a file does not open, try converting it to MP4 first or use the latest Chrome or Edge.

Is there a file size limit?

There is no fixed limit because nothing is uploaded, but processing uses your device's memory. Videos under about 100MB work smoothly on most computers; on phones, short clips (under 10 seconds) work best.

How do I make my GIF smaller?

Shorten the clip first, then reduce the output width, then increase the frame interval (e.g. 0.1s → 0.2s halves the number of frames). Changing FPS does not shrink the file: it only changes playback speed. In our test with the same 4.5-second clip, FPS 5, 10 and 20 all produced a 14MB GIF, while going from a 0.1s to a 0.2s interval cut it to 7.5MB.

Why does my GIF look sped up?

Playback speed equals frame interval × FPS. The defaults (0.3s × 10 FPS) play three times faster than the original. For real-time playback, set FPS to 1 ÷ interval, for example a 0.1s interval with 10 FPS.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes, it works in modern mobile browsers. Phones have less memory than computers, so keep clips short and the output width moderate for the best results.

Tips for a great GIF

Keep it between 3 and 10 seconds

Short GIFs load faster and loop more naturally. If your video is long, split the key moments into several short GIFs. Starting and ending on similar frames makes the loop look seamless.

Pick the right width

480–640px works well for social media and chat, 640–800px for blogs and docs, and 800–900px for portfolios and presentations. If most viewers are on phones, staying under 500px gives fast loading and crisp results. Avoid upscaling a low-resolution source.

Understand frame interval vs. FPS

The frame interval decides how often a frame is captured from your video, so it controls how many frames the GIF contains and therefore its file size. FPS only decides how fast those frames are played back. For real-time playback use a 0.1s interval with 10 FPS (smoothest) or 0.2s with 5 FPS for lighter screen recordings.

Make captions readable

Use high-contrast colors such as white text on a dark background, set the background opacity to around 0.5–0.7 so the video stays visible, and keep the text short – 30–50px usually reads well.
