How to use

  1. Add a video or photo

    Drop a video or photo (JPG, PNG, HEIC) on the box above or click to choose one. It's processed inside your browser and never uploaded.

  2. Pick the size and the moment

    Choose 16×16, 32×32 or 64×64 to match your display. Drag on the original to move the square that goes into the GIF, then set the start and length. The preview on the right loops the real result as you change things. Fewer colors give bolder, simpler pixel art.

  3. Make and save

    Press Make pixel GIF and save it. You get the original-size file and a large copy with each pixel scaled up.

Good to know

  • A photo becomes a single still piece of pixel art (PNG), with no motion. iPhone HEIC photos work as they are.
  • Show all keeps the video's own shape with no empty bars. A vertical video at 64 comes out as something like 36×64, with the long side at 64. If you need an exact square for your screen, use Fill.
  • 16×16 is only 256 pixels, so faces and text are hard to read. Big, simple shapes with a clear color difference from the background work best. Move the square while watching the preview.
  • Pixel animation is usually made at 12 frames per second, half of film's 24. 8 fps is the minimum that still reads as smooth, 4 fps is a deliberately choppy retro look, and 24 fps is overkill unless it's a fast effect like a splash. For a device, start with 12 fps and 2 to 4 seconds.
  • A frame that doesn't differ from the one before by a single pixel is merged into it and shown longer. Moments where motion stops get held, and you get fewer frames and a smaller file.
  • A plain resize just gives you a blurry mosaic. This tool follows the rules pixel artists use instead.
    • Colors from the whole clip are grouped into 8, 16 or 32, and every frame shares that one palette.
    • Each pixel takes the single color that covers most of it, not the average, so edges change cleanly from one pixel to the next.
    • Light colors shift slightly warm and dark colors slightly cool, and the darkest color is a deep navy instead of pure black.
    • Stray single pixels are cleaned up, and pixels that barely changed keep the previous frame's color to cut down flicker.
  • Pixel displays like Divoom can load a GIF from their app. Making it at the same size as the screen lets you see exactly which color each pixel will be. Apps may limit the number of frames or the file size, so if it won't load, shorten it or drop to 8 fps.
